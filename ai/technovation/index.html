What is Technovation?

Technovation is a global nonprofit program that helps young people learn to solve real problems with technology. Teams identify a challenge in their own community, design a solution, build a working prototype (such as a mobile app or an AI-powered tool), and present their idea to judges. Mentors support teams at every stage.

Its flagship program, the Technovation Challenge, is for young people ages 8 to 18. Teams compete in three age divisions (Beginner, Junior and Senior), the season runs October through May, and the program runs in 160 countries.

For official program details, rules and dates, see technovation.org (opens in a new tab).

Source: technovation.org: Technovation Challenge (opens in a new tab)

About the 2027 season

This season, Fremont-area girls will team up, pick a problem they care about in our community, and build a mobile app or AI-powered solution for it with help from mentors. At the end of the season, teams submit their project to the Technovation Challenge.

New for 2027: teams can build any working digital solution, not just a mobile app, and Technovation is adding a co-ed track alongside the Girls Track.

What teams build and submit

Teams learn from Technovation's free tech and AI curriculum, then build a working digital solution to a real problem in their community. It must use coding and/or AI, be tested with real users, and work. For 2027 that can be a mobile app, an interactive web app, a browser extension, a chatbot or AI assistant, an SMS or voice tool, a data dashboard, an automated workflow or AI agent, a Code.org App Lab project, a coded robot, drone or gadget, or (Beginner division only) a Scratch project.

The full Technovation Challenge curriculum is 30+ hours. Technovation says it generally takes about 40 hours in total for students to complete the program.

Every submission includes

  • One video, up to 4 minutes (new for 2027; it replaces the separate pitch and technical videos), on YouTube or Vimeo
  • Technical work: the code and/or a live link to the solution
  • Written pieces in English: project description, project development and a learning reflection
  • Senior: a business canvas. Junior: a user adoption plan

Mentors may help teams learn and find resources, but they may not create any part of the submission. Any use of AI tools must be disclosed.

How judging works

Every team that submits is judged by volunteer judges using a separate rubric for each division. There are three rounds:

  1. Quarterfinals: every team that submits is a quarterfinalist. Judging happens online or at in-person Regional Pitch Events, and semifinalists are announced by email.
  2. Semifinals: judged entirely online. Judges pick the Finalists and the Regional Honorees (Regional Honorees do not advance further).
  3. Finals (Girls Track): held at World Summit, where Finalist teams pitch to a panel of judges and one Grand Prize winner per division is announced.

For the new co-ed track, the number of finalists and any further honors will be decided in summer 2027.

Prizes & recognition (Girls Track)

  • 15 Finalist teams worldwide: 3 Beginner, 5 Junior and 7 Senior, chosen across Africa, Asia & Oceania, Europe, North America and Latin America
  • Finalists: a $500 educational stipend per person, plus free travel and accommodation to World Summit for all team members (worth more than $10,000 per team)
  • Grand Prize winners (one per division): $750 per person, in place of the $500 stipend
  • Regional Honors: 30 teams, with a $250 educational stipend per member

Stipends are unrestricted and meant to further each person's learning.

Mentors

A mentor supports one team for a full season: helping girls stay on track and manage their time, stay motivated, find answers, and get past setbacks. Mentors don't teach coding; the curriculum does that.

  • Who: teachers, parents or guardians, after-school coordinators, tech professionals, educators and college students. No technical background needed, just a willingness to learn alongside the team.
  • Time: about 30–40 hours in total: 1–2 hours of onboarding, then 2–4 hours a week with the team between October and April.
  • Screening: Technovation requires mentors to complete a background check during onboarding. [Any extra Fremont screening steps TBD]
  • You can co-mentor with a friend or colleague to share the work.
